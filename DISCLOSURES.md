Market Labs Provenance Standard — Disclosures

File: DISCLOSURES.md
Standard: Market Labs Provenance Standard
Version: 0.1.0
Status: Draft Standard

⸻

Purpose

This document defines the minimum disclosure requirements for Market Labs research, educational content, market commentary, tokenized experiments, referral relationships, sponsorships, and third-party references.

The objective is clarity.

A viewer, reader, researcher, or future reviewer should be able to determine:

* what the content is,
* whether the creator has a financial interest,
* whether compensation exists,
* whether a referral relationship exists,
* whether a third-party affiliation exists,
* and whether the artifact represents research, commentary, advertising, or an experiment.

Disclosures must describe reality accurately.

A generic disclaimer does not replace disclosure of a material conflict.

⸻

1. CORE DISCLOSURE PRINCIPLE

Disclose the relationship, not merely the disclaimer.

For example:

Weak:

Not financial advice.

Stronger:

The creator holds a position in the asset discussed. This recording documents an independent market thesis and may reflect that economic interest.

Where relevant, both may appear.

⸻

2. INDEPENDENT RESEARCH DISCLOSURE

Any Market Lab that studies a company, asset, platform, protocol, or third-party organization should clearly state whether the research is independent.

Standard field:

Independent Research: YES / NO / PARTIAL

Where YES:

This is independent research and commentary. The project is not represented as an official research unit of the referenced company or asset issuer.

⸻

3. AFFILIATION DISCLOSURE

Where a third-party brand or organization appears in the project name, title, thumbnail, repository, Discord server, or public-facing description, disclose whether an actual affiliation exists.

Examples include:

* Dell Technologies
* SpaceX
* NVIDIA
* Robinhood
* Solana
* GDex.pro
* universities
* government agencies
* historic sites
* publishers
* platforms

Recommended field:

Affiliation Status:

Example:

Independent project. No sponsorship, endorsement, ownership, or official affiliation with SpaceX is implied.

Do not describe an organization as a partner, sponsor, collaborator, or affiliate unless the relationship actually exists and can be supported.

⸻

4. CREATOR POSITION DISCLOSURE

For market-related artifacts, disclose whether the creator has a position in the referenced asset where that position is material to interpreting the content.

Recommended fields:

Creator Position:
NONE / LONG / SHORT / OTHER / NOT DISCLOSED
Position Present At Recording:
YES / NO
Position Present At Publication:
YES / NO / UNKNOWN

Where appropriate, more detail may be provided.

The standard does not require unnecessary publication of private account information.

The objective is conflict transparency.

⸻

5. ECONOMIC INTEREST

A creator may have an economic interest beyond a conventional long or short position.

Examples:

* ownership of a token,
* creator allocation,
* founder allocation,
* liquidity position,
* equity interest,
* revenue share,
* referral compensation,
* affiliate fees,
* sponsorship compensation.

Recommended fields:

Creator Economic Interest:
Affiliate Economic Interest:

If none exists:

NONE KNOWN

Do not leave a materially relevant relationship ambiguous.

⸻

6. REFERRAL AND AFFILIATE DISCLOSURES

If the artifact contains a referral link, affiliate code, copy-trading link, platform invite, or other compensated acquisition path, disclose that relationship near the relevant link.

Recommended field:

Referral Relationship: YES / NO

Example:

This description contains a referral link. The creator may receive compensation or another benefit if a viewer uses it.

Where exact compensation is known and material, it may also be described.

A referral link should not be presented as though it were a neutral research citation.

⸻

7. SPONSORSHIP AND PAID RELATIONSHIPS

If money, services, access, products, travel, credits, or another material benefit influenced the creation or publication of an artifact, disclose the relationship.

Recommended fields:

Sponsored:
Paid Promotion:
Material Benefit Received:
Sponsor:

Where none exists:

Sponsored: NO

Where a platform requires its own branded-content or paid-promotion disclosure, use the platform’s required mechanism in addition to the provenance record.

⸻

8. MARKET CONTENT PURPOSE

Every market-related artifact should identify its primary purpose.

Allowed initial values:

EDUCATION
RESEARCH
MARKET COMMENTARY
MARKET RECORD
EXPERIMENT LOG
TRADE REVIEW

Example:

Content Purpose:
MARKET RECORD

This field describes the artifact.

It does not determine its legal status.

⸻

9. MARKET RISK STATEMENT

Where appropriate, include a concise risk statement.

Example:

Markets are risky and outcomes are uncertain. This artifact documents research and commentary, not a guarantee of future performance.

For leveraged trading:

Leverage can amplify both gains and losses and may result in rapid loss of capital.

For highly speculative tokenized assets:

Tokenized or digital assets may be highly speculative, volatile, illiquid, or lose substantial value.

Risk language should be accurate to the subject.

Avoid boilerplate so broad that it becomes meaningless.

⸻

10. “NOT INVESTMENT ADVICE”

The phrase:

Not investment advice.

may be used where appropriate.

However, this standard explicitly rejects treating that phrase as a universal compliance shield.

It does not replace disclosure of:

* holdings,
* compensation,
* referral relationships,
* sponsorship,
* conflicts,
* misleading claims,
* or applicable legal obligations.

⸻

11. PRE-EVENT MARKET RECORDS

For pre-event analysis, preserve chronology.

Recommended fields:

Stage:
PRE-EVENT
Outcome Known At Creation:
NO

Where the creator has a position:

Creator Position:
LONG / SHORT / OTHER

The title and description should represent the artifact as a contemporaneous thesis or observation rather than imply a guaranteed future outcome.

Preferred language:

Premarket thesis

Pre-event market observation

Setup under review

Avoid unsupported certainty such as:

Guaranteed breakout

Easy money

This will moon

Can’t lose

⸻

12. POST-EVENT REVIEWS

A post-event artifact should not imply that later knowledge was available during the earlier thesis.

Where applicable, link the original artifact.

Example:

Reviews Artifact:
DELL-PREMARKET-2026-09-03-001

A successful outcome does not retroactively change the evidence class or certainty of the original thesis.

⸻

14. TOKEN LANGUAGE

Avoid describing token ownership as equivalent to:

* equity,
* debt,
* ownership in a company,
* guaranteed yield,
* guaranteed profit,
* contractual return,

unless such a claim is legally and factually supported.

Do not use the provenance standard itself to declare that a token is or is not a security.

That is a separate legal determination.

⸻

15. REFERENCE-ASSET DISCLOSURES

Where a Market Lab studies a reference asset but is not controlled by or affiliated with the issuer, make that distinction visible.

Example:

Project:
XLABS
Reference Asset:
SPCX
Affiliation Status:
Independent research project. No implied affiliation with SpaceX or related entities.

Likewise:

Project:
DELL Market Lab
Reference Asset:
DELL
Affiliation Status:
Independent research project. No implied affiliation with Dell Technologies.

⸻

16. AI DISCLOSURE

Where AI materially contributes to the artifact, the provenance record may identify that contribution.

Recommended fields:

AI Assistance:
Model:
Model Role:
Human Reviewer:

Example:

AI Assistance:
YES
Model:
ENOCH ONE / GPT
Model Role:
Research synthesis and structured analysis
Human Reviewer:
Justin Lee Goldston

Do not imply that AI independently verified a factual claim unless the underlying sources were actually checked.

⸻

17. MODEL AGREEMENT DISCLOSURE

If multiple AI systems reach similar conclusions:

Preferred:

Multiple models independently generated similar analytical recommendations under the supplied prompt conditions.

Avoid:

Five independent experts proved this conclusion.

Model agreement is not factual corroboration by itself.

⸻

18. HISTORICAL AND HUMAN-SUBJECT DIGNITY

Where content concerns deceased persons, enslaved persons, victims of violence, children, or other vulnerable historical subjects:

* represent sourcing accurately,
* preserve uncertainty,
* avoid sensationalizing suffering merely for engagement,
* distinguish historical fact from author interpretation,
* correct errors when new evidence emerges.

The provenance system may identify a historical person as an entity.

It should not reduce that person to a promotional keyword.

⸻

19. CHILDREN AND FAMILY CONTENT

Where a child appears in or is discussed in a Market Labs-adjacent artifact:

* avoid unnecessary disclosure of private information,
* avoid publishing sensitive identifying information,
* distinguish educational or documentary purpose from promotional use,
* comply with applicable platform requirements and law.

The provenance system should preserve only the minimum personal information necessary for the legitimate record.

⸻

20. EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTION REFERENCES

Mentioning a university, school, research institution, publisher, historic site, or other institution does not establish institutional sponsorship or approval.

Where necessary:

References to institutions identify locations, research context, public records, education history, or independently produced work. They do not imply institutional endorsement unless expressly documented.

⸻

21. SCREENSHOTS AND PLATFORM RECORDS

Screenshots should not be used in a way that misleadingly implies:

* endorsement,
* partnership,
* official recognition,
* verified ownership,
* or legal status.

Where context matters, preserve:

Capture Date:
Platform:
Original Context:
Artifact Source:

If a disclosure was materially incomplete or incorrect, correct it transparently.

Recommended fields:

Do not silently rewrite a material conflict or relationship after publication.

26. DISCLOSURE PLACEMENT

Not every disclosure belongs in every public title.

Use the appropriate layer.

GitHub provenance record

Full structured disclosure.

YouTube description

Material viewer-facing disclosures.

Spoken content

Material conflicts that are important to interpreting the content.

Discord

Relevant disclosure where research or market calls are presented.

Short-form social content

Concise disclosure with a link to the complete record where practical.

The full provenance record should remain accessible even when a public platform imposes space constraints.

⸻

27. GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

Disclose the relationship.

Do not rely on disclaimers to cure misleading content.

Holdings and incentives matter.

Referral links are economic relationships.

A reference asset is not the laboratory.

A laboratory is not automatically affiliated with the issuer.

AI agreement is not verification.

Preserve chronology.

Correct material errors transparently.

Research architecture does not determine legal classification.
